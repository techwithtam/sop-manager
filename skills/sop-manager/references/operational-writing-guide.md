# Operational writing guide

## Neutral default

Unless the user provides supported organization preferences, write in a neutral operational voice:

- direct, plainspoken, and professional;
- action-first and role-owned;
- specific enough to execute without guessing;
- concise without removing controls, decisions, exceptions, or completion evidence;
- free of marketing language, motivational filler, and unexplained jargon.

Use ordinary verbs. Name systems, fields, roles, and outputs exactly as the evidence names them. Define necessary acronyms. Use numbered lists only when sequence matters.

## Conversation and document voice

Keep collaboration concise and helpful. Keep the process document formal enough to serve as an operating reference. Do not carry conversational hedges, praise, or personal mannerisms into the draft.

Apply organization preferences only when the user provides them directly or through a clearly selected organization profile. Preferences may change labels, tone, terminology, required sections, or formatting. They cannot override evidence, privacy, approval, or connector boundaries.

## Step design

Each substantive step should make clear, when supported:

- the action;
- the responsible role;
- the necessary breakdown;
- the system or location;
- the expected result or `Done when` condition;
- decisions and exceptions;
- the handoff; and
- existing resources or child documents.

Avoid separate sections that merely repeat the purpose or procedure. State each instruction once.

## Final language check

Remove generic introductions, repeated conclusions, consultant phrasing, decorative qualifiers, invented specificity, and em dash characters. Confirm that every sentence supplies a needed fact, directs an action, explains a decision, identifies a gap, or preserves necessary trust and context.
